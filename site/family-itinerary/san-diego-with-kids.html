San Diego Itinerary With Kids: Realistic 1, 2 and 3 Day Plans

Researched with AI (Miles Rowan) · sources linked and dated · How we check

Three realistic plans for kids 4 and 8: one part of town a day, with a break built in. Swap stops for age, weather and your hotel.

Quick route decisions

How many days do you have?

One day The zoo and Balboa Park

Go early, take a break after lunch, and leave La Jolla and Coronado out.

Two days The zoo, then Mission Bay

Keep one big ticket day apart from one easy, no-ticket day by the water.

Three days Mission Bay, the zoo, then La Jolla

Use the third day for Birch Aquarium and the coast, or swap in the Coronado ferry.

Day-by-day routes

Your day-by-day plans

Our suggested order. Pick a plan, then check tickets, hours and your route for your dates.

One-day first look

Use this for

Short visits and first-time families who want the zoo without crossing the city

First

The San Diego Zoo at opening (9 a.m. listed November 1 to 25). Allow 4 to 6 hours including lunch (our estimate), and pick a few animals rather than every zone.

Then

A hotel break or shade after the zoo. Keep this even if the zoo runs long.

Last

A walk in Balboa Park or the hotel pool, only if everyone still has energy.

When to stop

Stop after the zoo and one easy finish. La Jolla, Coronado and a second ticket are the first things to leave out.

Two-day first trip

Use this for

Weekend families who want one big ticket day and one easy day by the water

Day 1: Zoo

Follow the one-day plan. Keep dinner close to your hotel.

Day 2: Mission Bay

A morning on the shore, with no entry ticket. The city lists swimming areas, playgrounds and public restrooms; choose your exact stop and check beach and bay status first.

Keep one break

Protect one free afternoon. If Day 1 runs late, drop the Day 2 extras before you cut food or rest.

When to stop

Don't add both La Jolla and Coronado to Day 2.

Three-day balanced trip

Use this for

Families who want a light first day, one zoo day and one La Jolla day

Day 1: Mission Bay or arrival

If you land today, keep it to the shore or the hotel, and buy snacks. Don't make this a full sightseeing day.

Day 2: Zoo

Follow the one-day plan. Zoo parking covers same-day re-entry, so a midday hotel break needs no second parking payment. Check the zoo's rules on coming back in with your ticket.

Day 3: La Jolla

Birch Aquarium for 1.5 to 2 hours (our estimate), then the coast nearby. Its free parking is limited to 3 hours. Or swap in the Coronado ferry, from ≈ $72 round trip for four, in place of Birch's ≈ $130.

When to stop

Keep one stop optional each day, and don't try La Jolla and Coronado on the same day.

Stop rules

Four rules for an easier day

One part of town a day

Keep the zoo and Balboa Park together, make Mission Bay its own easy block, and pick La Jolla or Coronado for the coast.

Schedule the break

Choose the meal, hotel return or nap before the day starts. If the break disappears, drop the next activity.

Decide what to drop

Move on only if everyone has eaten and the next stop can be skipped without losing a booking. A garden or pool is easier to drop than a timed ticket.

Already booked something?

Keep your bookings fixed and give each day one part of town. Cut cross-town extras first, and keep arrival and departure days light.

Common planning questions

San Diego family itinerary FAQ

How many days do families need in San Diego?

Two or three days work best for a first family trip. One day can work if you choose the zoo plus one easy finish instead of trying to see the whole coast.

What should families skip on a short San Diego trip?

Avoid stacking the zoo, La Jolla, Coronado and Mission Bay into one day. Parking, hills and drive time can take over the day.

What is the easiest rainy-day San Diego itinerary with kids?

Use Birch Aquarium or a Balboa Park museum as the main stop, keep meals nearby, and save the beach or zoo for the best weather.
