Plan the day with kids

  • Opening day: Hunter's pages we read give no winter opening date yet, and its status page showed 0 lifts open on Nov 15; check it before you drive.
  • Lessons: Hunter lists kids' lessons for ages 3-4, 5-6 and 7-14. Child group lessons are for ages 7 to 14; younger kids go in smaller groups by ability. Kids must be potty trained.
  • Bring: A helmet for every child in a lesson: Hunter requires them and doesn't rent them for lessons.
  • Childcare: Hunter's childcare page says childcare is not available. Its PlayCare page wouldn't open for us, so ask Hunter before you count on it.
  • Parking: Free same-day parking; arrive early for the closest spots, especially on weekends and holidays. On weekends a shuttle runs from the farther spots. The North lot, about 2 miles west, has no ticket sales or rentals.

Lift tickets and lessons: We haven't read Hunter's ticket and lesson prices, so check live. Epic Pass holders save 20% on group lessons, Hunter says. See Hunter lift ticket FAQs.

From Hunter home page, Hunter first-timer lessons (read Nov 10), Hunter childcare, Getting to Hunter and Hunter lift and terrain status, read Nov 15.

Getting there from New York City

  • Car: about 2½ hours from New York City (Hunter's estimate): I-87 to exit 20 or 21, then about 45 minutes.
  • Bus: Adirondack Trailways runs daily from Port Authority to Hunter and back, Hunter says; call (800) 776-7548 for times and fares.
  • Train: Amtrak to Hudson (about 2 hours) or Metro-North to Poughkeepsie (about 1½ hours), then about an hour by taxi; Hunter says ride-share is scarce in Hudson and suggests booking ahead from Poughkeepsie.

If the snow is thin

  • Snowmaking: Hunter calls itself the snowmaking capital of the world but doesn't give a coverage figure on the page we read.
  • Snow tubing: Hunter has a tubing park; we can't read its Hunter snow tubing page, so check hours and ages there.
