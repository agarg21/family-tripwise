# Family Tripwise review: site, SEO and operating model

Nov 11, 2026 · @Apoorva Garg

## Verdict

The operator is well built and honest, and its output has not yet turned into readers. Since October 27 it made 2,213 commits and added 41 pages. Search clicks went from 11 to 16 per 28 days, and average position has stayed near 44 since October 1. Two weeks is too short to judge the new pages, and the owner is willing to give them a few months.

Waiting will not fix two things. No outside site links to this one, and every dated page adds upkeep whether or not anyone reads it. The plan below keeps improving pages continuously, and moves effort toward what creates visits.

Five things hold the site back, in order:

1. **No authority.** A search for the domain finds no outside mention. Position has not moved in six weeks while indexed pages tripled.
2. **Upkeep grows with every page.** About three-quarters of commits touch no public page. Much of that is re-checking dated facts.
3. **Social is not working.** 38 tracked posts earned 1,154 views, 1 like, 0 follows and 0 link clicks.
4. **The deals product is mostly theme-park passes.** Six of the seven promoted deals are season passes for families who live nearby. Deal pages have earned one click.
5. **Pages are long, caveat-heavy and light on photos.** Key pages run 14 to 28 phone screens. The word "confirm" appears 381 times across the site.

What is working: itinerary and stay-area pages rank between position 7 and 25 and earn most of the clicks. They are among the oldest pages, but age is not the whole reason. Things-to-do pages went up the same day and sit at position 46, so the easier searches are winning. The new holiday city pages reached positions 7 to 16 within a week, on small impression counts.

There is no reader analytics and no email list. At about 16 clicks a month neither would show much, so both are low priority for now.

## Content

The facts are careful and the page tops are clear, but pages are too long and too hedged for a parent on a phone.

**What is good**

- **Deal cards are easy to read.** [The deals hub](https://familytripwise.com/deals/) shows "Without ≈ $720 · With ≈ $420 · Save ≈ $300" for a named family and visit count.
- **Prices hold up.** The SeaWorld Orlando Fun Card at $104.99 matches [SeaWorld's offers page](https://www.seaworld.com/orlando/limited-time-offers) today.
- **Holiday pages carry real substance.** [Christmas in NYC with kids](https://familytripwise.com/holidays/new-york-city-christmas-with-kids.html) lists 13 outings with dates, free options and ticket cost for four.
- **Itineraries answer the question first.** [The Las Vegas itinerary](https://familytripwise.com/family-itinerary/las-vegas-with-kids.html) opens with cost, a day plan and the catch.

**What holds it back**

| Problem | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Pages are very long on a phone | Homepage 14 screens, NYC hotels 15, NYC Christmas 17, This Week 28 |
| No photo on the first phone screen | True on 5 of the 6 pages viewed. The site has 27 unique hero photos across 73 pages, many labelled as older. |
| Caveats still crowd the copy | "Confirm" appears 381 times sitewide and 38 times on the NYC hotels page. "No price shown" appears 102 times. |
| Checking notes leak into reader copy | A SeaWorld San Diego deal page says the end date came from "the Black Friday banner image file name". |
| Hotel headline prices are loose | [NYC hotels](https://familytripwise.com/where-to-stay/new-york-city-family-hotels.html) prices come from Google Hotels with "room type, taxes and fees not shown". |
| The homepage does not sell the site | It opens with a paragraph and a run of text links. It gives no reason to trust the site or come back. |
| Nothing is first-hand | Pages say "we have not visited". That is honest, and it is also the gap against every ranking competitor. |
| The About page is stale | It still describes a five-city scope. The site covers eleven. |

The hedging pattern has one cause. The operator publishes what it could not confirm instead of leaving it out. A parent needs the answer and one caveat, with the rest behind "How we checked".

## Technical SEO

The technical base is sound, and it is not what limits ranking. Four fixable gaps remain.

| Area | Finding | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Pages respond in 0.2 to 0.4 seconds. HTML is 6 to 14 KB compressed, with 4 KB of script. | Good |
| Indexing | 72 of 74 priority URLs indexed as of November 9. Sitemap lists 77 URLs with no errors. | Good |
| Canonicals | Every page has a correct self-canonical. | Good |
| Internal links | Every sitemap URL has at least 34 inbound links. | Good |
| Structured data | 47 of 80 pages have none, including every deal, holiday and This Week page and the homepage. No page has Article, Organization, Breadcrumb or Event markup. | Gap |
| Existing markup | The 33 marked-up pages use ItemList and FAQPage. Google has shown FAQ rich results only for government and health sites since 2023. | Low value |
| Titles | 55 of 80 titles run past 60 characters and will be cut off in results. | Gap |
| Expired deals | 5 ended deal pages are still in the sitemap and indexable, each saying "we stopped listing this check". | Gap |
| Sitemap dates | 40 of 77 URLs show November 10 as last modified. Daily re-renders change the date without a real content change. | Gap |
| Footer links | A holiday page carries 153 links, 114 internal, mostly the same footer block on every page. | Minor |

Event markup on the holiday pages is the most useful of these. Those pages hold dated events with prices and places, which is exactly what that markup describes.

GitHub Pages cannot send a real redirect or a "gone" response. Ended deal pages should therefore be folded into a durable page per brand, such as one SeaWorld passes page, and dropped from the sitemap.

## Policy risk

Risk is moderate and comes from one gap: no human reviews what gets published.

- **Human review.** [Google's generative AI guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), updated October 1, 2026, says AI content should be manually fact-checked and reviewed before publishing. The About page states that "a separate AI reviewer checks material changes". That is the one place the site clearly falls short of the guidance.
- **Scaled content.** [Google's spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) targets many pages made to rank that add little for readers, however they are made. The site's pages are distinct and sourced, so exposure is low today. It rises if page count keeps growing faster than usefulness, and ended deal pages are the weakest part.
- **The Miles Rowan byline.** Every page says "Researched with AI (Miles Rowan)" and the profile says "AI employee". That is disclosed, so keep it. Remove the personality lines from the profile, and do not give Miles a face or credentials.
- **Author markup.** No page names an author or publisher in structured data. Add the organization as publisher and the founder as the accountable person.
- **Affiliate disclosure.** No affiliate or sponsored links exist on the site, so nothing is needed now. Add a disclosure beside the link the day one goes live.
- **Savings claims.** Pass savings depend on an assumed number of visits. The cards say so, which keeps them honest. Keep that line on every card and post.

I could not confirm some statements about Google's guidance from my first review, so they are left out here.

## Strategy

Shift effort from producing fresh items to earning attention for fewer, better pages. Two weeks of growth-first output has not moved clicks, and the project's own November 3 research already advised analytics, an email list and data-led press before scaling.

**The site serves two readers, and can keep both.** Hotel lists and itineraries serve families travelling to a city. Season passes and most holiday outings serve families who live there. Neither has to come first. They compete only for the homepage message, the social feed and the operator's hours. Local content expires fastest, so it should get a fixed share of those hours.

**Double down**

- **Itineraries and stay-area pages for the existing eleven cities.** They are the only page types near page one. Deepen these before adding anything new.
- **The nine holiday city pages as permanent yearly pages.** Keep the year-free URLs. This season they feed social. Refreshed from September 2027, they can rank.
- **Venue-level holiday answers.** Demand is by venue: "busch gardens christmas town" 18,100 searches a month, "nybg holiday train show" 8,100, "bronx zoo holiday lights" 4,400, all low difficulty. Add a clear "what it costs a family of 4" block per major venue inside the city pages.
- **One asset worth linking to.** The dated hotel price data across ten cities is original. Turn it into a single study of what a family of 4 pays for a holiday weekend, and pitch it city by city.

**Stop or shrink**

- **One page per pass deal.** Fold them into one durable page per brand that answers "is the pass worth it for a family of 4". Brand deal searches are steady: "great wolf lodge deals" gets 12,100 a month.
- **Five to six social posts a day.** Reels average about 2 seconds of watch time, and Instagram reports 91% to 99% of viewers leaving within 3 seconds. Post once a day at most until one format holds attention.
- **New sections and cities.** The ski page and London were added while core pages sit at position 45 and below.
- **"First to publish" as a goal.** Official pages and large deal sites already hold those results. Speed helps only once people follow the site.

**Change**

- **Put visit-creating work first.** That means the pages nearest page one, the first outside links, and the cheap technical fixes.
- **Install analytics as a baseline, and leave email for later.** Analytics takes ten minutes. An email signup can wait until visits reach the hundreds a month.
- **Add a weekly human review.** It closes the policy gap and recalibrates the AI reviewers.
- **Hold the November 12 go/no-go for real.** Queue item Q14 already schedules it. Use the numbers in this review.

## Working style

The operator is disciplined about facts and blind to results. It is steered by a work queue and its own reviewers, and nothing in the loop tells it whether a parent read, clicked or came back.

**What is strong**

- **Evidence rules are real and enforced.** Prices carry dates and a basis, expired items drop automatically, and 226 test files guard them.
- **Approval tiers are sensible.** New URLs, noindex changes, redirects and all outside actions need an owner row in `ops/OWNER.md`.
- **It recovers from its own failures.** Deploy breaks on November 10 were diagnosed and fixed within hours.
- **The research is good.** The November 3 growth memo reached most of this review's conclusions.

**What to change**

| Issue | Evidence | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity is measured, not outcomes | The scoreboard counts releases per week and indexed pages. Clicks are one line. | Lead the scoreboard with clicks, pages in the top 20, signups and referring sites. |
| Most work is bookkeeping | 1,640 of 2,213 commits since October 27 touched no public page. | Run hourly only for deal and trend watching. Publish in two or three batches a week. |
| The upkeep load keeps growing | The state file lists dozens of dated re-read obligations. Every new page adds more. | Cap live deal pages. Give each page a re-read budget, and retire pages that earn nothing. |
| The reviewers grade their own side | A LEGOLAND page passed at 4.0 while you could not tell spend from save. | Review five pages yourself each week and compare your scores with theirs. |
| Quality bars moved down to keep shipping | Decisions D24, D28 and D34 lowered the pass mark from 4.0 to 3.5. | Hold the bar. Ship fewer pages. |
| Your involvement is high, and on the wrong things | More than 30 owner decisions in 15 days, mostly approving output. | Spend 30 minutes a week reading pages and choosing priorities. |
| The best advice sits on the deferred list | Analytics, newsletter and press are under "Not now". Title tests and stub removal are too. | Install analytics as a baseline. Start the press work now. Revisit email when visits reach the hundreds a month. |
| No check on what search engines see | Tests cover facts and layout. None compares live noindex, canonical, status codes and robots before and after a deploy. | Add that comparison to the deploy. |

**Useful points from the article you sent**

[Theo's SEO and GEO playbook](https://x.com/theoagi/status/2120163220312699025) is a summary of other people's advice, with rules of thumb, not tested numbers. Five points apply here.

- **Slow down on a new domain.** It suggests 1 to 4 new pages a week and 2 to 3 months before judging a change. The site added 41 pages in 15 days.
- **Set up Bing Webmaster Tools.** The repo has no mention of Bing. The article says Copilot runs on Bing and ChatGPT has leaned on it.
- **AI search crawlers can already read the site.** I tested OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Bingbot. All get a normal response, and www redirects correctly.
- **Track AI answers by hand.** Write 15 to 30 real parent questions and log monthly which sites the assistants cite. Your own research found AI Overviews on 10 of 12 result pages.
- **Find where links come from.** "Best of" lists and unlinked mentions are its main link sources. Pitching family travel roundups fits the hotel price study.

Its advice to skip FAQ markup matches the finding above. Its warnings against city-by-service template pages and mass AI posts are the same risk named under Policy risk.
