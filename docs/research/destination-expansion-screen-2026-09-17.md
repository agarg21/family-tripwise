### Cancun: Concrete Information Gain And Its Limits

The [current competitor guide](https://www.famtravelclub.com/blog/cancun-family-resorts) describes the Alltra/Sunscape Family Junior Suite as sleeping two adults and three children. The [current Hyatt room page](https://www.hyattinclusivecollection.com/en/resorts-hotels/sunscape/mexico/cancun-resort-spa/accommodations/rooms-suites/) lists the Family Junior Suite with king, bunk and trundle bedding but occupancy **four guests**. This is an unresolved published-source conflict, not proof of actual capacity, a verified policy change, or a finding that every competitor is wrong. Do not route a family of five to this room as confirmed. A later evidence pack must resolve the exact room/category, child-age counting, booking channel and dated availability, or leave the option conditional.

A [public resort-selection question](https://www.reddit.com/r/AllInclusiveResorts/comments/1qnx347/recommendations_for_family_friendly_resorts_in/) asks for one resort serving a toddler and an older child, with a nightly budget excluding flights and airport transfer inclusion. Its page displayed a relative age of eight months; exact publication date was not independently verified. This is one qualitative task, not demand or prevalence. A useful comparison needs exact party fit, age-specific provision, inclusions and a complete same-date total; generic family-friendly labels cannot answer it. No commenters' recommendations, identities or safety assertions are retained.

Geographic discovery shows that the Cancun label can include the Hotel Zone and resorts marketed under Riviera Cancun/Playa Mujeres or the wider Riviera Maya. Expedia's base guide also includes Isla Mujeres and Puerto Morelos. Preserve these entity distinctions in research rather than multiplying indexable destination pages. Exact URL overlap across the resort/base/regional query families remains UNKNOWN until a controlled SERP batch is collected.

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** a small source-dated comparison that exposes unresolved occupancy and inclusion conflicts will answer a family booking decision better than a generic scored resort list. Current result: plausible, not validated by users. Reject the need for a generic new comparison table; retain the narrower evidence-normalization hypothesis. Next test: three candidate properties, one four-person and one five-person task, current first-party room/club/inclusion checks, and a comparison against representative ranking pages. No new page is authorized by this finding.
