[Google's method](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en) describes sampled, normalized search interest, not absolute volume; low-volume terms can display zero, and noise is especially important for low-interest series. Comparisons have separate normalization scales and can change with sampling. No ratios of these indices are interpreted as search-count ratios. [DataForSEO's coverage explanation](https://dataforseo.com/help-center/no-search-volume-data-for-some-keywords) identifies Google Ads restrictions affecting many child-worded terms. That is consistent with our missingness pattern, not proof of the reason for any individual missing row. Both documentation pages retrieved September25; no new provider measurement implied.

## Current Query And Product Check

September25 live web search for `things to do in Orlando with kids itinerary family` surfaced official tourism, editorial activity lists, commercial itinerary/ticket guides and community results. This was a qualitative search sample, not a controlled US rank export; no exact positions or overlap percentage claimed. Two representative page bodies inspected:

- [Visit Orlando](https://www.visitorlando.com/plan/vacation-ideas/visiting-with-kids/): age-specific routes plus theme-park, water-park and beyond-park discovery. Its official destination authority and broad inventory are not advantages we can claim.

A generic age-filtered list or list of rainy-day options is not enough information gain. Its attraction judgments were not adopted as verified facts.

Retrieval September25; publisher update dates not established. Competitor structure is qualitative evidence, not proof of satisfaction or representative market coverage. The next falsifiable task should ask whether a mixed-age family can choose a non-park/rest day under explicit time, weather, admission and transport constraints without unsupported route assurances. We have not yet demonstrated that current alternatives fail that task; reject immediate generic-list implementation.
