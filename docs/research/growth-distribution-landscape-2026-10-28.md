## 0. Bottom line

1. **Disney deals are crowded. Non-Disney family deal math is not.** MouseSavers, Undercover Tourist, Disney Tourist Blog, WDWInfo and The Points Guy already cover Disney and cruise promos. None of them routinely publishes **the actual total for a family of 4 on named dates, with occupancy rules, fees and real availability checked**. Our evidence-layer doctrine is built to do exactly that.
2. **The best proof of the gap** is the Slickdeals thread on Great Wolf Lodge's "$24 per person" suites (Oct 2025). The rate was "based on 2 guests per room". Several commenters could not find it at any date or location. The real family figures they reported were $104–$136 a night on weekdays and $300+ on weekends. That kind of fine-print correction is a repeatable post format.
3. **"5–6 posts/day" should mean 5–6 total publishable units a day, spread across platforms, not 5–6 on each platform.** That volume is normal on X, Threads, Bluesky and Pinterest. On Instagram (counting Stories) and Facebook it is high but workable. On TikTok and YouTube Shorts it is wasteful and risky: TikTok caps API posts at about 15 a day per creator, its median views stay flat at high volume, and YouTube demonetizes "inauthentic" templated content.
4. **Owned distribution comes first.** Start an email newsletter now. Kit is free up to 10,000 subscribers; beehiiv is free up to 2,500. Social reach rents attention. Email is the only channel we own.
5. **API access is easier than it looks for a single-brand publisher.** Meta (Instagram, Facebook Page, Threads) needs no App Review when the app only serves a business we own or manage. YouTube's official docs (updated 2026-10-08) no longer force unverified-project uploads to private. Bluesky is free and open. The real friction points are:
   - TikTok's audit: unaudited direct posts are private-only.
   - Pinterest's Trial-to-Standard upgrade: Trial pins are sandboxed, and the upgrade needs a demo video.
   - X's pay-per-use pricing: $0.015 per post, or $0.20 if the post has a link.
   - Google OAuth "Testing" mode: refresh tokens expire after 7 days.

### 1.2 The gap Family Tripwise can own

**Positioning: "Family Deal Check".** Each promo is turned into a verdict for a real family, built from the evidence layer we already maintain.

What each check contains:
- **The official promo terms, captured on a date.** Example: GWL "$24/person, based on 2 guests per room; 21+ guest required; multiple-night stay may be required; blackout periods".
- **A family-basis quote on that date.** Example: 2 adults + 2 kids aged 4 and 8, for the named dates. Show room, unit and currency, and say what is or is not included in taxes and fees.
- **An availability sample.** Example: "Rate found on 3 of 12 date/location combos checked on 2026-10-28."
- **The hidden costs that matter to families:** resort/port fees, kid-age cutoffs, 3rd/4th-guest rules, occupancy caps, minimum nights, kids-club and water-park access hours.
- **A labelled verdict:** Worth it / Only Sun–Thu / Skip for families with X.

Why this is defensible:
- The TPG kids-cruise guide states "taxes, fees, port expenses ... must be paid separately" but gives no family total.
- Disney Tourist Blog tells readers to "do the math for yourself".
- In the Slickdeals GWL thread:
  - commenters could not find the advertised rate in VA, NC or PA;
  - one saw $34/pp instead of $24;
  - a family of 4 in Naples, FL paid $116/night on weekdays versus $300+ on Fri/Sat.
- The work is mechanical, repeatable and source-backed, which matches `AGENTS.md`.

Where to focus:
- **Under-served deal families:** indoor water-park resorts (Great Wolf, Kalahari), kids-sail-free cruises (Royal Caribbean, NCL 3rd/4th guest), Legoland/Sesame Place/regional parks, ski "kids ski free" programs, national-park and state-park family passes, hotel family suites, and school-break airfare windows.
- **Disney:** do not compete on Disney code discovery. Only cover Disney when we can add family-total math. Those posts must be marked Disney-specific under the destination rules.
- **Events and trends:** new attraction openings, school-break calendars, holiday-event dates, new family hotel openings. These are timely but must stay evidence-labelled.

### 1.3 Monetization options, in order of fit with the evidence brand

1. **Affiliate links on site pages and newsletter**, never as the reason a deal is recommended. Cookie windows as reported by Stay22 (third party):
   - Booking.com 30 days, Expedia 7 days, Viator 30 days, GetYourGuide 31 days, Skyscanner 30 days.
   - Stay22 aggregates hotel and OTA programs.
   - Klook runs creator storefronts.
2. **Newsletter ad networks and recommendations:** beehiiv Ad Network on paid plans (beehiiv says it pays creators >$1M/month), Kit Recommendations (Creator plan), Substack Recommendations.
3. **Direct sponsorship** once the list reaches mid-five figures.
4. **Premium alerts later** (Going/Thrifty/Travelzoo model), e.g. a paid "family deal alerts for my home airport and dates" tier. Thrifty Traveler's switch from credit cards to subscriptions is the precedent.
5. **Avoid credit-card affiliate as a core model.** It pulls content toward cards and away from family fit.

**FTC compliance applies to every affiliate post.**
- Put the disclosure next to the link or recommendation, in plain words ("We earn a commission if you book through this link").
- In video, the disclosure goes on screen and/or in the spoken track, not only in the description.
- A link-in-bio or profile disclosure is not enough.
- Platform paid-partnership tools do not replace our own disclosure.

### 2.2 Rules on automated and AI content, labels, and spam penalties

**Instagram, Facebook and Threads (Meta)**
- Meta auto-labels detected AI images ("AI info").
- Since **2026-04-30**, accounts that **regularly repost content they didn't create** lose recommendation eligibility for photos and carousels. Reels already had this rule.
- Original content (designed by us, or materially transformed with commentary or unique text) is fine. Screenshots of other people's posts and watermark-only edits are not.
- Implication: **design our own cards from our data. Never repost promo graphics or hotel photos.**

**TikTok**
- AIGC labels are required for realistic AI scenes, people or voices.
- Labels are not needed for AI-assisted captions or scripts, stylized graphics, or slideshows of real photos with text overlays.
- C2PA metadata triggers labels automatically.
- Undisclosed realistic AI is escalated: label, then removal, then strikes.
- Branded content has to use the commercial-content toggle. API clients must expose it.

**YouTube**
- Creators must disclose **realistic** altered or synthetic content.
- Since **May 2026**, YouTube auto-applies the label when its systems detect significant photorealistic AI. On Shorts the label is an overlay.
- The label alone does not affect recommendations or monetization.
- Separately, the **"inauthentic content"** monetization rule (effective 2025-07-15) targets mass-produced, near-identical videos. YouTube's own example is "slideshows that all share the same narration". A templated deal Short with the same voice-over script every day is at risk.

**X**
- Automation is allowed for informational content.
- Duplicate or substantially similar posts, auto-posting on trending topics, and bulk reposting are prohibited.

**Pinterest**
- Repeating the same image or URL in a short window gets flagged as spam and loses distribution.
- Tailwind's guidance is 15–25 pins/day, mostly fresh. A Tailwind study found more than 50/day can hurt.
- Changing only the title or description does not make a pin "fresh".

**API vs native posting**
- No platform documents a reach penalty for posting via API. The evidence is thin and comes from vendors.
- The real penalties are content-based: unoriginal content, duplicates, spam.

## 3. Formats that grow fastest for this niche

### 3.1 What the data says (Buffer, 45M+ posts, published 2026-03-19)

**Instagram**
- Carousels get the **highest engagement** (6.9% of reach), ahead of single images (4.4%) and Reels (3.3%).
- **Reels get the most reach:** 2.25× single images and 1.36× carousels.
- So use Reels to reach new people and carousels to deepen engagement with followers.

**Other platforms**
- **TikTok:** video engagement is 3.39%, against 1.92% for photo/carousel posts (+77% for video).
- **Threads:** video 5.55% > image 4.55% > text 2.79% > link 2.34%.
- **X:** text 3.56% ≈ image 3.40% > video 2.96% > link 2.25%. Links are penalized everywhere.
- **Facebook:** formats sit within one percentage point of each other; images lead at 5.2%.

**Benchmarks:** Socialinsider's 2026 engagement-by-followers figures are TikTok 2.5–3.7%, Instagram ~0.45%, Facebook ~0.13% and X ~0.10%.

### 3.2 Format plan for a deal check

| Asset | Built from | Platforms |
|---|---|---|
| **Deal Check carousel** (4–6 slides): headline verdict → advertised vs family-of-4 total → fine print checklist → availability sample → who it's good for / skip if → source and date slide | Rendered from a verified deal JSON (HTML template → Playwright screenshot → **JPEG 1080×1350**) | IG carousel, TikTok photo post (inbox draft), Threads carousel, FB album, LinkedIn not needed |
| **Short video (15–35s)**: the same slides with motion, plus on-screen text and a voice-over that **changes every time** (the specific catch for that deal) | ffmpeg/Remotion from the same slides. The owner (or a human voice) records VO where possible; AI voice is acceptable but must not be identical script to script | IG Reel, TikTok, YouTube Short, Threads/FB video |
| **Text thread / post**: "Advertised $24/pp. For 2A+2K on Sun–Tue Nov 9–11 we saw $X/night incl. … Fri–Sat: $Y." Link in a reply where link reach is penalized | Deal JSON | X, Threads, Bluesky |
| **Pin** (2:3 vertical) linking to the evergreen site page (e.g. the destination's family hotel shortlist) rather than the expiring deal | Site data | Pinterest |
| **Newsletter block**: the week's top 3–5 checks, plus "deals that failed the family test" | Deal JSON | Email |

### 3.3 Minimum production needs, and AI-imagery rules

- **Carousels, images, pins and text can be generated fully programmatically** from verified data with no platform rule against it. Typographic, data-card, map and chart styles are "original" under Instagram's April-2026 rule and need no AIGC label, because they are not photorealistic.
- **Do not generate AI images of real hotels, rooms, pools or kids.**
  - They would mislead families about a real property, which contradicts the evidence doctrine.
  - They trigger AI labels and auto-labels on Meta, TikTok and YouTube.
  - Use official imagery only where licensed (press kits often allow editorial use; check the terms), or use maps, icons and our own photography.
- **Video:** a slide-plus-motion template is fine. Vary the narrative per deal to stay clear of YouTube's "inauthentic content" risk.
- **API limitations to plan around:**
  - Instagram API images must be JPEG and hosted on a public URL (we can host on familytripwise.com).
  - Branded-content tags and shopping tags are not supported via the IG API.
  - Trending audio is effectively a native-app action, so use TikTok's inbox/draft flow for audio-dependent posts.

### 5.2 Deliverability basics (Gmail/Yahoo, enforced since 2024)

- **Authenticate the sending domain:**
  - Publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC, with alignment to the From domain.
  - Use a subdomain such as `news.familytripwise.com` so newsletter reputation is isolated from the main domain.
- **One-click unsubscribe** (RFC 8058 header). Honor opt-outs within 2 days. All four platforms above handle this.
- **Spam complaint rate below 0.3%**, aiming for under 0.1%. The bulk-sender threshold is ~5,000 Gmail messages/day, but the rules apply at any volume.
- **Opt-in only.** No purchased lists. Use double opt-in if early signups come from social giveaways.
- **Warm up.** Send consistently (weekly), start with the most engaged readers, and remove subscribers who have not opened in 90+ days.
- **Content:** a plain, recognizable From name ("Family Tripwise"), few links, no link shorteners, and text-heavy rather than image-only emails.

### 5.3 Cadence

- **Weekly "Family Deal Check"** (Thursday is a good slot for weekend trip planning).
- **Plus at most 1–2 "flash" alerts a month**, sent only when a verified deal is exceptional and time-boxed.
- Each issue includes a "failed the family test" section, which builds trust.
